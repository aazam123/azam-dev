Financial Report Details

1: Current Assets

This section includes all assets that are expected to be converted to cash or consumed within one year from the reporting date. Current assets are presented in order of liquidity.

Key components include cash and cash equivalents, trade receivables, inventories, and other short-term assets.

1: Cash and Cash Equivalents

Cash and cash equivalents comprise cash on hand and demand deposits, together with other short-term, highly liquid investments that are readily convertible to a known amount of cash and which are subject to an insignificant risk of changes in value.

This includes bank balances, petty cash, and short-term investments with maturities of three months or less from the date of acquisition.

2: Trade Receivables

Trade receivables are amounts due from customers for goods sold or services rendered in the ordinary course of business. They are initially recognized at fair value and subsequently measured at amortized cost using the effective interest method.

An allowance for doubtful debts is maintained to cover potential losses from uncollectible receivables.

3: Inventories

Inventories are measured at the lower of cost and net realizable value. Cost includes all costs of purchase, costs of conversion, and other costs incurred in bringing the inventories to their present location and condition.

Net realizable value is the estimated selling price in the ordinary course of business, less the estimated costs of completion and the estimated costs necessary to make the sale.

4: Non-Current Assets

Non-current assets are assets that are not expected to be converted to cash or consumed within one year from the reporting date. These assets are held for use in the production or supply of goods or services, for rental to others, or for administrative purposes.

They are initially recognized at cost and subsequently measured using various accounting policies depending on the nature of the asset.

4: Property, Plant and Equipment

Property, plant and equipment are tangible items that are held for use in the production or supply of goods or services, for rental to others, or for administrative purposes and are expected to be used during more than one period.

They are initially recognized at cost and subsequently measured at cost less accumulated depreciation and accumulated impairment losses.

5: Intangible Assets

Intangible assets are identifiable non-monetary assets without physical substance. They are initially recognized at cost and subsequently measured at cost less accumulated amortization and accumulated impairment losses.

Common examples include patents, trademarks, software, and goodwill.
